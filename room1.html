College Student Room │3D Model │ Environmental & Prop Design

#Blender

A 3D bedroom scene modeled in Blender, inspired by a combination of my university dorm and my old room back home. The project began as a collection of studies of everyday objects and gradually developed into a complete interior. Furniture, electronics, clothing, and personal belongings come together in a slightly cramped, lived-in space, using small details and intentional disorder to suggest the personality and routines of its occupant.

Challenge

The challenge was to bring a varied collection of individually modeled props together in a convincing bedroom environment. The project began with observing and recreating real objects around me, providing opportunities to practice different shapes, construction methods, and surface details. As the collection grew, the focus expanded from individual models to how they could work together within one space. Furniture and electronics needed structured forms, while clothing, curtains, and other fabric elements required softer, less regular surfaces. Another challenge was balancing detail with readability: the room needed to feel crowded and personal while still allowing individual objects to be appreciated. Inspired by my university dorm and my room back home, the aim was to capture the experience of fitting everyday life into a single room, where studying, relaxing, getting dressed, and pursuing hobbies all share the same limited space.

Process

Development began with modeling everyday objects, gradually expanding into furniture, desk accessories, clothing, and decorative elements. Different modeling approaches were used across the collection. Mirror modifiers supported symmetrical forms, while Array modifiers handled repeated elements. Subdivision Surface modifiers refined curved objects, including the mug and ashtray, and combinations of Bevel, Solidify, and subdivision helped develop rounded edges and surface thickness across other meshes. Fabric studies included cloth simulation setups for curtain and draped surfaces, while the rug combined a particle system with subdivision to introduce surface texture. A Geometry Nodes setup was also used for the fairy lights. Materials combined BlenderKit assets that I adapted and UV mapped with textures created from image sources, allowing wood, fabric, plastic, metal, and smaller printed details to contribute to the room’s variety. A procedural moonlit skydome was created with shader nodes for the view through the window. As the environment developed, object placement became part of the storytelling, with cables, loosely folded clothes, and everyday clutter suggesting ongoing use. Lighting and camera composition brought these separate studies together for the final Cycles renders.

Solution

The finished project is a detailed bedroom environment that presents a broad collection of original prop models within a shared setting. Larger furniture establishes the room’s layout, while smaller objects give each area its own purpose and character. The desk brings together electronics and everyday accessories; clothing and fabric elements soften the surrounding structure; and personal belongings, including the guitar, plants, and printed details, suggest the occupant’s interests. Small placement decisions help the room feel temporarily unoccupied rather than carefully staged: a cigarette remains in the ashtray, cables emerge beneath the desk, and the chair is turned toward the door as though someone has just stood up. The final presentation includes different lighting treatments, closer views, and a solid-shaded overview, showing both the atmosphere of the environment and the modeling behind it. Together, these views turn a series of individual object studies into a cohesive showcase of prop modeling, material work, and environmental storytelling.
