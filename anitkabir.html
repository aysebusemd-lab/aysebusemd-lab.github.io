    Anıtkabir: The Revival | Game Design & Development | 3D Modeling

    A first-person historical puzzle game developed in Unity, inspired by Anıtkabir, the mausoleum of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk in Ankara, Turkey. The experience combines exploration, historical storytelling, and interactive puzzles, allowing visitors to discover the stories of fallen soldiers and uncover forgotten memories throughout the monument.

    Course(s)

    1.3 Fusion Challenge 2

    Category

    Game Design, Unity Development, 3D Environment Design, 3D Modeling, Interactive Storytelling, Puzzle Design

    Link(s)

    Playable game:  Anıtkabir: The Revival on itch.io

    ​

    Gameplay overview:  6-page overview of the game

    Duration

    12 weeks

    Challenge

    The challenge was to design and develop an interactive 3D ghost museum for a fictional theme park. The museum needed to feature a unique collection of ghosts with their own stories, filmed and integrated into a virtual environment. Visitors had to be able to explore freely, interact with objects and characters, and experience atmospheric lighting and visual effects. With this brief in mind, the concept took a historical approach, exploring how a monument could become a more interactive and engaging experience without losing its cultural significance. This led to Anıtkabir: The Revival, a first-person puzzle game set in Anıtkabir, the mausoleum of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk. Rather than using ghosts for horror, the game introduces the spirits of fallen soldiers whose stories unfold through exploration and memory-related tasks. The aim was to encourage visitors to engage with the history of Anıtkabir on a more personal level, combining historical storytelling with puzzles and environmental interactions while remaining respectful of the monument's significance.

    Process

    Development began with research into Anıtkabir's architecture, history, and symbolism, followed by concept planning and 3D modelling in Blender. The original assets were then imported into Unity to build the Lion Road, Hall of Honor, and Projection Room environments. Most of the development focused on programming the gameplay systems in C#. These included proximity-based interactions, an inventory and object-retrieval system, and a contextual dialogue manager with different responses based on the player's progress and carried items. Additional scripts handled task completion, puzzle validation, scene transitions, and the conditions for triggering different endings. Custom UI elements, interaction sounds, and cinematic sequences were implemented using Unity Timeline, camera animations, CanvasGroups, and TextMeshPro. Several technical issues were addressed along the way, particularly managing player controls during conversations and cutscenes. The environment was also refined through lighting, atmospheric effects, and Shader Graph experiments, including a custom wind animation for tree leaves that weren't responding to Unity's default wind settings. For the Projection Room, an original historical video was produced through costume preparation, filming, and editing in Adobe Premiere Pro before being integrated into Unity as an interactive projection.

    Solution

    The finished project is a first-person historical puzzle game set in a fully modelled recreation of Anıtkabir, with three main areas to explore and two possible endings. The experience begins on the Lion Road, lined with 24 lion statues, ten of which can be interacted with to discover historical facts. A mechanic inspired by the pathway's symbolism discourages visitors from looking too far upward while walking, temporarily stopping movement and displaying a red vignette to simulate stumbling. Inside the Hall of Honor, three ghost soldiers share their stories and ask visitors to recover personal belongings: a medal, a photograph, and a revolver. These tasks can be completed in any order, with the soldiers responding differently to correct items, incorrect items, or empty hands. A password-protected door leads to the final area, with an optional hint directing players toward the dates engraved on Atatürk's memorial. In the Projection Room, visitors activate a projector to watch an original live-action historical video. The experience concludes with either a Good Ending, featuring a fireworks sequence, or a Neutral Ending, depending on whether all three soldiers' tasks were completed. The result combines historical storytelling, exploration, and interactive puzzles into a museum experience where learning comes from actively engaging with the environment rather than simply observing it.
