#Blender

An ongoing 3D product advertisement bringing together my Zooble-inspired eyeshadow palette and a modeled, rigged version of the character from The Amazing Digital Circus. The project expands the Piece by Piece packaging concept into a character-led sequence, combining product presentation with expressive physical acting.

Challenge

The challenge is to develop a product advertisement in which character performance and product presentation support one another. The palette already has a detailed visual identity, so the animation needs to give it a narrative context while leaving enough space for its design to be appreciated. Zooble’s asymmetrical construction introduces another challenge: the character combines rigid geometric pieces, narrow limbs, curved components, and different hand shapes, each requiring an appropriate approach to movement. Translating a human performance into this unusual body means adapting poses and gestures while preserving their intention and readability. The project also involves coordinating several stages of production, from reference recording and movement planning to modeling, rigging, and animation, with repeated adjustments as the character is tested in more demanding poses.

Process

Development began with preparing the character and product for animation in Blender. I modeled Zooble’s contrasting body parts, recreating the triangular head, mismatched eyes, colorful torso, and varied limbs to establish a recognizable silhouette. The character was then rigged with controls for posing the body, face, and individual appendages. Particular attention has gone into how the different components respond to movement, balancing flexible sections with pieces that need to retain their shape. Pose testing has helped identify areas requiring further refinement, including bends, joint connections, and the behavior of the character’s more unusual limbs. To plan the performance, I recorded live-action reference of myself kneeling beside a container, searching inside it, and making broad arm gestures. Selected frames were arranged into a visual sequence, with simplified drawings over the photographs to study body position, changes in balance, and the direction of each action. These drawings reduce the movement to its main shapes, making it easier to translate the performance into Zooble’s proportions. In Blender, a simple box provides a reference for the character’s interaction and helps establish contact, reach, and staging. The current work focuses on developing clear poses and refining the rig alongside the animation. The existing palette model, including its controllable opening lid, provides the product asset for the developing advertisement.

Solution

The project is currently in production, with a modeled and rigged character, a prepared product asset, recorded acting reference, and initial interaction poses established. At this stage, the work demonstrates the progression from a static packaging concept toward an animated presentation, alongside the testing needed to make an unconventional character perform convincingly. Further development will focus on refining movement, timing, character–object contact, and the product reveal before completing lighting and rendering. The intended result is a short fan-made advertisement that brings the palette’s graphic identity and Zooble’s character performance together within the wider cosmetics project.
