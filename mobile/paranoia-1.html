Paranoia [ DEMO ] │Game Design│Prototype Development

#Unity

Paranoia is a first-person psychological horror game set in a late-1990s supermarket. Inspired by a true story, the game follows Ashley, a young cashier, whose ordinary evening takes a disturbing turn. With a focus on anxiety, social pressure, and unreliable perception, the game explores the uncertainty of whether danger is real or a product of fear.

Challenge

The original assignment for Principles of Game Design was to develop an original game concept, document its design in a Game Design Document (GDD), and build a playable prototype demonstrating its main mechanics, visual style, and gameplay. Development followed several milestones covering concept creation, story, game world, player interactions, and level design. The project was later revisited during an additional Game Design course at ELVTR, with the aim of developing the initial concept into a more complete and structured GDD. This involved applying professional game design principles to the game's vision, Mechanics, Dynamics & Aesthetics (MDA), gameplay loops, systems, level design, narrative, and balancing.

The chosen concept was Paranoia, a psychological horror game where the challenge was to create tension without relying on combat, monsters, or conventional jump scares, instead using player interactions and social situations to make ordinary encounters feel increasingly uncomfortable.

Process

Development began with the game's concept, story, characters, and PSX-inspired visual direction. A supermarket environment was assembled in Unity using sourced 3D assets, with custom interactions, dialogue, and gameplay systems developed in C#. The initial prototype focused on the core experience: basic cashier tasks, environmental inspection, dialogue choices, and a breathing mini-game triggered by stressful encounters. These systems were developed alongside the game's narrative to make Ashley's anxiety part of the gameplay rather than something explained through dialogue alone. During the ELVTR course, the original GDD was expanded and reworked using the MDA framework, gameplay loops, a Composure system, branching narrative paths, and more detailed level designs. Feedback from instructor Gavin Yeo helped refine the relationship between the game's story and mechanics, with particular attention to player agency, emotional pacing, and how the design could eventually be implemented.

Solution

The project resulted in a playable Unity prototype and a 37-page Game Design Document covering the larger game concept. The demo introduces the player to Ashley's routine through a short cashier training sequence before allowing exploration of the supermarket. Inspectable objects reveal her inner thoughts and personal background, while a dialogue system introduces choices during conversations with NPCs. A breathing mini-game turns moments of panic into an interactive challenge, requiring the player to maintain control as the encounter becomes more stressful. The game uses low-poly PSX-style visuals, dark lighting, and retro-inspired UI to establish its unsettling atmosphere. The prototype focuses on testing these core systems and how they work together to communicate the game's psychological themes. The expanded GDD develops the concept further, introducing a Composure system that affects perception, dialogue, and player control, alongside branching storylines, multiple endings, and a three-act narrative structure. Level layouts, character profiles, environmental storytelling, game loops, and audio direction were also documented to guide future development. The project received an ELVTR Certificate of Completion for Exceptional Performance, along with a recommendation letter from instructor Gavin Yeo, highlighting the game's worldbuilding, narrative integration, and application of game design principles.
