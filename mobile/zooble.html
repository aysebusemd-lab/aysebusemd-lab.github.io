Piece by Piece (Eyeshadow Palette) | 3D Product Design | The Amazing Digital Circus Fan Art

#Blender#Photoshop#Illustrator

A fan-made eyeshadow palette inspired by Zooble from The Amazing Digital Circus, combining vector illustration, packaging design, and 3D modeling. Every graphic element was created manually in Adobe Illustrator, assembled into packaging artwork in Photoshop, and applied to a palette modeled in Blender. Titled Piece by Piece, the concept translates Zooble’s mismatched shapes, contrasting colors, and distinctive personality into a cohesive cosmetic product.

Challenge

The challenge was to translate an existing character into an eyeshadow palette with its own visual identity. Zooble’s contrasting colors, irregular shapes, and assembled appearance offered a starting point, but these references needed to work across several different formats: the outer cover, interior artwork, shade arrangement, and three-dimensional packaging. The design needed to feel expressive and deliberately mismatched while maintaining a readable hierarchy for the product name, shade names, and supporting information. Another challenge was carrying the same visual language between Illustrator, Photoshop, and Blender. Individual illustrations had to function as parts of a larger composition, while the completed packaging needed to remain convincing when wrapped around a model and viewed from different angles. The aim was to create a complete fan-designed product whose connection to Zooble extended beyond simply placing the character on its cover.

Process

Development centered on building a collection of original graphic elements in Adobe Illustrator. These included character-inspired shapes, decorative motifs, patterns, and supporting symbols, allowing the packaging to draw from Zooble’s appearance throughout its design. The title Piece by Piece connected the product to the character’s assembled form and informed the fragmented, collage-like composition. Contrasting animal prints, stripes, grids, colorful shapes, and torn-paper effects were brought together in Photoshop to create the packaging artwork. A dark base provided contrast for the brighter elements and helped the typography remain readable within the layered design. The interior continued this visual language around the character image and the arrangement of 18 named shades, combining direct color references with playful names and varied surface appearances. The completed artwork was then transferred to Blender, where I modeled the palette’s case, lid, and recessed eyeshadow pans. Materials helped distinguish the printed packaging from the powder surfaces, with variations suggesting matte, shimmer, and glitter finishes. I also modeled Zooble, recreating the character’s asymmetrical silhouette and contrasting components as part of the project’s wider visual presentation. The palette lid was connected to an Empty controller so it could be opened and closed around its hinge, making it possible to present the exterior and interior through the same model. Open and closed product renders brought the illustration, packaging composition, and modeled form together.

Solution

The finished concept is an 18-shade eyeshadow palette with a visual identity built around contrast, fragmentation, and playful disorder. The outer cover introduces Piece by Piece through bold pink lettering and a layered collection of patterns and character-inspired elements. Opening the palette reveals an interior that carries those same motifs around the character artwork and shade layout, creating continuity between the outside and inside of the product. The colors, names, and varied powder finishes extend the theme into the makeup itself, making the shade collection part of the design. The 3D presentation demonstrates how the flat artwork works as a complete object, showing the packaging’s thickness, the recessed pans, and the relationship between the lid and base. The controllable lid supports both open and closed views and provides a foundation for further presentation work. The result brings individually created vector assets, Photoshop composition, and Blender modeling together in one finished product concept, showcasing a workflow that moves from illustration to packaging and three-dimensional visualization.
